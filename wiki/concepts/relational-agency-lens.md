# Relational Agency Lens

Analytical frame treating children's agency as **distributed** across relationships and institutions—not reducible to single-user tool design.

---

## Origin

Proposed in [[agency-lit-review-hal]] discussion (§5.3) to address mismatch between:

- Evidence that agency is socially situated (peers, parents, teachers, platforms)
- Recommendations focused mainly on single-user controls

Draws on psychology/sociology (Bandura, Emirbayer & Mische) and CCI participatory work.

---

## What it foregrounds

- Social identities in context (e.g., student–teacher hierarchy)
- Who facilitates access to technology and interprets AI literacy
- Power asymmetries amplified by surveillance or opaque automation
- Institutional actors who can enable or override children's choices

---

## Design questions (from Brod et al., via review)

When designing for learners' agency, ask:

1. **What** can be chosen?
2. **Who** has choice?
3. **When** can choices be made?
4. **Where** can choices be made?

Assumptions surfaced here should be tested with children and facilitators.

---

## Documentation guidance

For child-facing AI features, document:

- Roles beyond the child (parent, teacher, moderator, platform)
- Whether agency is individual, dyadic, or collective
- What happens when adult intervention overrides child plans
