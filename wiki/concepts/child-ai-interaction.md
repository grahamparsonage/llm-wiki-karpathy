# Child–AI Interaction

Children's engagement with AI-mediated systems—knowingly or unknowingly—including generative tools, recommenders, voice agents, educational copilots, social VR moderation, and data-driven platforms.

---

## Scope in ingested literature

[[agency-lit-review-hal]] includes systems relying on **data-intensive decision-making**: LLMs, robots, social media/recommenders, smart home devices, creative coding copilots, embodied moderators.

Excludes (from that review's criteria):

- Non-AI HCI without data-driven decisions
- Children's agency only as AI system *designers* without interaction focus
- Agency in choosing AI *topics* without child–system interaction

---

## Why agency matters here

- Half to three-quarters of 9–17-year-olds use AI tools weekly (surveys cited in review)
- Algorithmic systems shape content, safety, and choices behind the scenes
- Children's cognitive and metacognitive capacities still developing
- Adult-mediated control (parents, teachers, policy) can limit agentic practice while aiming to protect

---

## Design strategy clusters (from review)

| Strategy | Intent |
|---|---|
| Control | Child retains ultimate decision; override safety tools |
| Choice | Meaningful, legible options—not overload |
| Scaffolded literacy | Understand who processes data and how systems decide |
| Active participation | Co-design and governance, not passive consumption |

---

## Research gaps (2026 review)

- Few studies with agency as primary focus
- Weak evaluation instruments; mostly exploratory observation
- Transformative/critical agency mostly speculative—not longitudinal praxis
- Cognitive/developmental accounts underrepresented vs sociocultural
- Single-user tool focus; weak attention to relational/institutional context
