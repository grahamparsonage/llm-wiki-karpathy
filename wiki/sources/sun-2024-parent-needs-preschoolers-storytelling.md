# Parent's Needs for Children-Centered AI Storytelling

CSCW qualitative study (N=17 parents, children 3–6): real-world use of AI storytelling tools and gaps vs parent–child interactive reading expectations.

## Key findings

- AI storytelling tools feel immersive/engaging but **fail parents' expectations** (interactive + algorithmic gaps)
- Parents want AI to **reduce burden** while supporting parent–child interactive storytelling—not replace parents
- ~63% of agent-mediated reading interactions driven by parents (redirecting attention, clarifying prompts)
- Prior work: CAs improve comprehension but lower productivity, lexical diversity, topical relevance vs human partners
- Technical limits: weak comprehension questions, poor turn-taking, sequential pre-generated questions without coherence
- Design implication: **human–parent collaboration** team, child-centered AI with parent navigation (preschoolers lack AI literacy)

---

## Relevance to child–AI interaction

Centres **relational agency**: children's effective agency in AI storytelling depends on parent mediation; critiques child-only evaluation of storytelling agents.
