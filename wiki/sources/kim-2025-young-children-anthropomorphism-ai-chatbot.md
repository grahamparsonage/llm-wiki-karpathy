# Young Children's Anthropomorphism of an AI Chatbot

fNIRS study (N=23, ages 5–6): anthropomorphism, engagement, and prefrontal activation during collaborative storytelling with an LLM chatbot, parent, or both.

## Key findings

- Three conditions: AI-only, parent-only, AI + parent; post-session anthropomorphism interviews
- Children anthropomorphised parents more than the AI overall; AI still scored relatively high on **perceptive** and **epistemic** attributions
- Anthropomorphism not linked to conversational turn count (engagement proxy)
- Right **dmPFC**: higher perceptive anthropomorphism ↔ greater activation (AI-only); lower activation when parent co-present
- Exploratory: higher dmPFC activation (AI-only) correlated with higher end-of-session “scared” mood
- **Parent co-presence** may help children interpret and regulate novel AI interactions

---

## Relevance to child–AI interaction

Links mind perception and neural engagement to **trust**, over-reliance, and **co-use** design for early childhood LLM chatbots; supports parent–AI joint storytelling rather than child-alone use.
