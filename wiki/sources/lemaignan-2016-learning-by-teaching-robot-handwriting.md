# Learning by Teaching a Robot — Handwriting

Nao “bad writer” robot taught by children with handwriting difficulties; learning-by-teaching shifts role from underperformer to expert.

## Key findings

- **Learning-by-teaching**: child as teacher, robot as naïve learner with configurable (simulated) poor handwriting
- Improves handwriting practice plus **self-esteem** and motivation via social role reversal
- Robot embodiment enables believable skill levels peers cannot fake; extends prior WoZ Nao verb-teaching with **autonomous** robot learning
- **Protégé effect**: child commits to robot’s success; strong affective bonds possible
- Adults remain essential: prompting feedback and orchestration; child rarely effective as teacher alone at ages 5–6
- Ethical note: child-as-mentor to robot understudied vs protégé-focused literature

---

## Relevance to child–AI interaction

Foundational **agency-through-teaching** case: child exercises epistemic authority over an AI/robot agent; informs tutor/tutee roles and adult scaffolding in child–robot educational systems.
