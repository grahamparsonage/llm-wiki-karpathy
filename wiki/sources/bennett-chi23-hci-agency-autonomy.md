## Main contribution

Agency and autonomy currently function as **vague umbrella constructs**, not well-functioning **boundary objects**—flexible across communities but lacking robust shared meaning. Only ~36% of papers give explicit definitions; many use terms interchangeably.

---

## Four aspects for organising HCI work

Framework for clarifying approaches (papers may span multiple):

| Aspect | Categories |
|---|---|
| **Self-causality & identity** | Executing, decision, self-congruence, self-construction |
| **Experience & materiality** | Material enaction vs experiential sense-of-agency |
| **Time-scales** | Micro-interaction (seconds), episode (hours), life (days+) |
| **Independence & interdependence** | Individual autonomy vs socially distributed agency |

---

## Etymological distinction (not always maintained in HCI)

- **Autonomy** (Greek *autos* + *nomos*): self-governance
- **Agency** (Latin *agere*): to set in motion, do, perform—emphasis on self-causality

Modern usage often treats them as near-synonymous; entanglement is acknowledged in philosophy and SDT/METUX.

---

## Implications cited by child-AI literature

- Lack of definitional clarity hampers cumulative knowledge and replicability
- Future HCI should specify which aspect(s) a design targets
- Ethical tensions: autonomy vs safety for children and diminished capacity (balance, not binary grant/deny)
