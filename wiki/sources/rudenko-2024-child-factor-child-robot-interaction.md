## Key findings

- CRI must combine **what robots can do** with **what children need** developmentally
- Surveys education and healthcare CRI; notes robots fit early childhood (anthropomorphism, animacy) but long-term efficacy often unproven
- Tension: robot-centred work optimises platforms in ad-hoc studies; human-centred work may reduce robot to “expensive tablet”
- Two long-term success factors: **developmental stage** and **individual characteristics**
- Age-conditioned designs should align robot roles/tasks with dominant developmental goals per period
- Proposes experiment designs embedding individual-difference factors for sustainable, engaging CRI

---

## Relevance to child–AI interaction

Generalises beyond robots to **child-centred** AI/HRI: one-size-fits-all personalisation fails; developmental alignment supports agency and meaningful participation.
