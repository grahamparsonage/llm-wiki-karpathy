# Children as Digital Technology Users

Primary audience segment for agency-centred child–AI documentation: minors interacting with AI-mediated systems in everyday, educational, and play contexts.

---

## Demographics (from ingested sources)

- Ages studied: roughly **3–17** across CCI corpus; teen-focused in many AI-safety papers
- Heavy generative-AI use among 9–17-year-olds (surveys: ~50–75% have used tools like ChatGPT)
- Settings: home, school, co-design workshops, social VR, kindergarten through secondary

---

## Goals

- Express preferences and creativity without undue system steering
- Understand how AI/recommenders affect what they see and do
- Participate in design and critique of technologies affecting them
- Develop critical stance towards datafication and platform power
- In CE contexts: shape—not only consume—technological futures

---

## Pain points

- Algorithmic drift ("zombie" passive consumption on TikTok-style feeds)
- Opacity about data processing and who decides
- Over-reliance on AI copilots eroding sense of originality
- Adult/institutional override of self-set plans (parents, schools)
- Resignation about irreversible data footprints
- Safety tools that feel punitive or surveillant before dispute is possible

---

## Expertise and support needs

- **Developing** metacognition, forethought, self-regulation (varies by age)
- Benefit from **scaffolded literacy**, not assumption of innate full agency
- Need **meaningful choice** with visible consequences—not choice overload
- Often require **facilitators** (teachers, parents) aligned with relational agency

---

## Implications for documentation

- Define which age band and developmental assumptions apply
- Name facilitator roles and override paths explicitly
- Distinguish child's agency from system's autonomy
- Avoid deterministic framing of AI outcomes as inevitable
